# IntegraFin 90-Day Growth and Operations SOP

> **First 30 days:** Execute the merged [IntegraFin 30-Day Full SEO, AEO, Local and Growth SOP](./INTEGRAFIN_30_DAY_FULL_SEO_GROWTH_SOP.md). It is the authoritative daily plan for August 10–September 8, 2026 and supersedes conflicting first-month instructions in older SEO roadmaps.

> This SOP turns the strategy audit into an operating system. It does not replace tax, legal, licensing, professional-standards, privacy, or cybersecurity advice. A qualified CPA/EA, counsel, and security lead must approve claims, credential language, referral arrangements, tax-service boundaries, and data-handling practices before launch.

## 1. The decision: what IntegraFin will focus on

For the next 90 days, do not market every possible service to every possible buyer. Run only these two acquisition motions:

### Motion A — SMB bookkeeping and tax readiness

Target owner-led U.S. service businesses and real-estate businesses, beginning in Katy/Fort Bend, that:

- Have 2–25 employees or approximately $250,000–$5 million in revenue.
- Use QuickBooks Online, Xero, Wave, spreadsheets, or an incomplete bookkeeping workflow.
- Have books that are behind, unreliable, or not ready for a tax, lender, payroll, or planning deadline.

**Front-door offer:** Books-to-Tax-Ready Assessment  
**Next offers:** Bookkeeping Cleanup Sprint or Monthly Close package

### Motion B — CPA/bookkeeping firm capacity

Target U.S. CPA and bookkeeping firms with 3–30 professionals that have seasonal backlog, cleanup work, bookkeeping production needs, or limited internal capacity.

**Front-door offer:** 20-minute CPA Capacity Audit  
**Next offer:** A tightly scoped 30-day CPA Capacity Pod pilot

### Approved positioning

**Headline:** Clean books. Confident filings. A finance team you can reach.

**Core promise:** IntegraFin helps U.S. small businesses and accounting firms clean up books, close books monthly, prepare tax-ready records, and keep deadlines visible through a documented U.S.-facing workflow.

Only publish claims that IntegraFin can prove. Never guarantee savings, deadlines, filing acceptance, audit results, IRS outcomes, or credentials.

## 2. Success at day 90

The first 90 days are successful only if all five conditions are met:

1. One tracked assessment funnel reliably turns suitable prospects into proposals.
2. At least three real, permissioned proof assets are published or ready to publish.
3. The CRM reports each lead from source through signed revenue.
4. One local/organic channel and one partner/outbound channel are producing qualified opportunities.
5. Package-level revenue, delivery cost, gross margin, capacity, and turnaround are understood.

Do not treat impressions, followers, traffic, or raw leads as the primary result.

## 4. Non-negotiable controls before promotion

Complete these controls before asking prospects to submit records or before scaling promotion:

- Use a secure client portal for tax returns, SSNs, bank information, payroll records, IDs, and financial documents.
- Keep sensitive-data warnings on every public form. Public forms may collect only basic contact and qualification information.
- Require MFA, unique user accounts, least-privilege access, managed passwords, backups, and documented access removal.
- Maintain and approve a Written Information Security Plan and incident-response procedure.
- Verify who holds each CPA, EA, PTIN, EFIN, or other credential and what work each person may perform or review.
- Identify the U.S.-facing engagement owner and named reviewer for every engagement.
- Obtain written client permission before using a name, logo, quote, result, screenshot, or case study.
- Clearly label illustrative examples; never present them as completed client work.
- Ensure commercial email uses accurate identity and subject lines, includes a physical postal address and opt-out, and honors suppression requests.
- Do not pay referral fees or share professional fees until counsel and the relevant credential holder approve the arrangement.

Any privacy, security, credential, or unsupported-claim concern is a stop-work issue and must be escalated immediately.

## 7. Lead response SOP

### Trigger

A form, call, email, WhatsApp message, referral, scheduler booking, or outbound reply is received.

### Procedure

1. Create or update the CRM record immediately.
2. Record the source, segment, requested service, consent/suppression status, and message.
3. Assign a sales owner.
4. Respond within 15 business minutes during published business hours. If this is not possible, send an acknowledgement with the expected human-response time.
5. Do not request sensitive records. Direct the prospect to the secure portal only after fit and access requirements are confirmed.
6. Ask only enough questions to decide whether to book a discovery call.
7. Create a dated next task before leaving the record.

### Triage outcome

- **Book discovery:** Likely fit and a real problem/timing signal.
- **Request basic non-sensitive clarification:** Fit cannot yet be determined.
- **Nurture:** Suitable later, but no current issue or timing.
- **Refer/decline:** Outside service, jurisdiction, credential, risk, economics, or capacity limits.
- **Escalate:** Tax deadline, notice, credential question, complaint, security issue, or sensitive data shared improperly.

## 8. Discovery and qualification SOP

Use FITS for every prospect:

| Dimension | Required discovery |
|---|---|
| Fit | Entity, service, software, industry, geography, and professional/jurisdiction requirements |
| Issue | What is behind, unreliable, risky, costly, or urgent |
| Timing | Filing, lender, payroll, close, hiring, season, or other decision deadline |
| Scope/economics | Entities, accounts, months, volume, states, records, approver, budget, SLA, and capacity |

Ask:

1. What prompted this conversation now?
2. What is the last month or report you trust?
3. Which entities, states, accounts, payroll providers, and payment platforms are involved?
4. What decision or deadline depends on the work?
5. What has already been tried?
6. What does “done” mean?
7. Who approves the scope, access, and payment?
8. What security, confidentiality, reviewer, or service boundary matters most?

At the end of the call, summarize verified facts, unknowns, dependencies, risks, and the next step. Do not promise price or timing until the required scope and records are known.

### Qualification decision

- **Qualified:** All four FITS areas are sufficiently known, the work is within approved scope, and delivery capacity exists.
- **Diagnostic needed:** Fit is likely, but a material file review is required to determine scope.
- **Nurture:** Fit exists without current timing or authority.
- **Decline/refer:** Work exceeds credentials, jurisdiction, risk tolerance, capability, or realistic budget/capacity.

Record the decision and reason in the CRM on the same day.

## 9. Assessment and proposal SOP

### Books-to-Tax-Ready Assessment

1. Complete the 15-minute triage.
2. Send a basic records checklist through an approved channel.
3. If file access or material analysis is required, issue and obtain acceptance of the paid diagnostic scope before work begins.
4. Exchange records only through the secure portal.
5. Review the agreed entities, periods, accounts, reconciliations, payroll, loans, and payment platforms.
6. Document unresolved items; never guess.
7. Deliver the health score and written scope memo within two business days after all agreed access and records are received, unless a different written deadline applies.
8. Recommend one next step: self-correct, Cleanup Sprint, Monthly Close, tax-scope review, professional escalation, or no engagement.

### CPA Capacity Audit

Document backlog by service, volume, turnaround, handoff, reviewer, access, confidentiality, white-label boundaries, end-client contact rules, QA, escalation, pricing, and pilot success criteria.

### Proposal requirements

Every proposal must contain:

- Situation and objectives.
- Entities, periods, accounts, and deliverables included.
- Exclusions and professional-service boundaries.
- Client responsibilities and records required.
- Timeline, dependencies, and definition of complete records.
- Named engagement owner, reviewer, and escalation path.
- Communication cadence and response expectations.
- Secure data workflow and access rules.
- Price, payment, assumptions, expiration, and change-control procedure.
- Acceptance and next step.

The Sales Owner may not alter price, scope, deadline, or security terms without the appropriate approval.

### Proposal follow-up

Use a limited, useful cadence:

- Day 2: Confirm receipt and answer scope questions.
- Day 5: Address the main decision risk or unknown.
- Day 10: Confirm timing and decision process.
- Day 14: Close the active loop or move to a dated nurture task.

Stop immediately after an opt-out or explicit decline.

## 10. Onboarding and delivery oversight SOP

### Day 0 — Signed scope

- Confirm payment terms, engagement owner, reviewer, communication channel, deadlines, dependencies, and client responsibilities.
- Create the client in the portal and practice-management system.
- Grant least-privilege access and record access owners.
- Send the records checklist and kickoff date.

### Day 1–7 — Access and completeness

- Confirm all agreed systems, records, entities, periods, accounts, payroll, loans, and payment platforms.
- Record missing items and assign an owner and due date.
- Escalate scope differences or deadline risk; do not silently absorb extra work.

### Day 14–30 — First visible win

- Deliver the agreed baseline, cleanup summary, first close, or pilot output.
- Provide an unresolved-items list, decisions needed, and next deadline.
- Review output using the documented QA checklist and named reviewer.
- Hold the 30-day client check-in.

### Monthly

- Deliver the agreed close package and open-item list by the contracted date.
- Record on-time status, rework, scope changes, client delays, and capacity impact.
- Notify the client of the next deadline and required records.

### Quarterly

- Review KPIs, service fit, client satisfaction, future deadlines, scope, price, and expansion needs.
- Do not sell advisory work until the underlying monthly close is dependable.

### Annually

- Review scope, pricing, margin, access, security, tax readiness, renewal, and appropriate review/referral timing.

## 11. Client health and escalation SOP

Assign every active client a health status each month:

- **Green:** Records arrive on time, no critical unresolved items, client responds, payment is current, and satisfaction is at least 8/10.
- **Amber:** Repeated late records, scope creep, delayed close, payment friction, low engagement, or recurring open items.
- **Red:** Tax/payroll deadline risk, security concern, complaint, material rework, unresolved professional issue, or churn signal.

### Required action

- Green: Continue; request a neutral review or referral only after a measurable win.
- Amber: Create a written corrective plan, owner, and deadline; discuss scope or process with the client.
- Red: Notify the Delivery Owner immediately; involve the Credentialed Reviewer or Security Owner where relevant; pause affected work if continuing would increase risk.

## 14. Review and referral SOP

Ask only after a real service win, such as a completed cleanup, dependable first close, or successful handoff.

1. Confirm there is no open complaint, deadline risk, security issue, or material rework.
2. Ask neutrally; do not request only positive reviews or discourage critical feedback.
3. Do not provide an incentive unless counsel and the relevant professional rules approve it and required disclosures are made.
4. Record the request date and outcome in the CRM.
5. Obtain separate written permission before converting feedback into a website testimonial or case study.
6. Respond to public reviews without exposing client status, tax facts, or confidential information.

## 17. Capacity, scope, and stop rules

### Capacity rule

Before signing work, the Delivery Owner confirms available hours, reviewer capacity, secure access, deadline feasibility, and the effect on existing clients. No sales target overrides a professional, security, or delivery limit.

### Change-control rule

When an entity, period, account, volume, deadline, deliverable, record condition, or client responsibility differs from the signed scope:

1. Stop the affected additional work.
2. Document the difference and delivery impact.
3. Issue a written change order covering scope, price, timing, dependencies, and approval.
4. Resume only after approval.

### Marketing stop rules

Pause or stop when:

- A campaign creates privacy, credential, professional, security, deceptive-claim, or outreach-compliance risk.
- Two documented learning cycles produce predominantly poor-fit leads.
- Response time or delivery quality falls below the agreed standard.
- Paid acquisition cannot be connected to qualified opportunities and signed gross profit.
- A package is unprofitable after including delivery, review, software, rework, and acquisition cost.
